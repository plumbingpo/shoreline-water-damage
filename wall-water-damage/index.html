Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Shoreline

Drywall acts like a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

What It Looks Like

  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Drywall seams ridging up and screw pops appearing across the surface
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.
