Kitchen Water Damage in Shoreline

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Shoreline homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Refrigerator Water Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
