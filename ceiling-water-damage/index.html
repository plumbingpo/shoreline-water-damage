Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Shoreline

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Signs to Look For

  • Popcorn or knockdown texture that flakes, drops or turns darker
  • Water dripping from a light fixture, vent or smoke detector
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Above

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.

Sudden Collapse

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. An entire piece can fall from a bulging spot onto anything below.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Because moisture runs along framing, plumbing and ductwork, the drip may be feet away from the entry point. We check beyond the visible stain so soaked sections down the line are not overlooked.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Wet insulation is removed along with any drywall that cannot stay. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.
