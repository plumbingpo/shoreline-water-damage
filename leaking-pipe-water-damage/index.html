Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Shoreline: What to Know

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Shoreline finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. That is our part of the job. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Inside the Wall

Mold thrives in a wall cavity that is both damp and dark. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.
