Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. It is not the repair. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Shoreline, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. They are two phases. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

How We Handle Water Damage Mitigation

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
